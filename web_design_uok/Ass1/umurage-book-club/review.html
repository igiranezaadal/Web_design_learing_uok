Book of the Month · October

A school on the hill, and a country holding its breath

Reviewed by Aline Uwase

Scholastique Mukasonga's novel Our Lady of the Nile takes place in a girls' boarding school high above the source of the Nile, in the early 1970s. The pupils are the daughters of ministers, officers and businessmen, sent there to become the country's elite.

On the surface, the book is a story of school life: lessons, uniforms, visits from important guests and secrets whispered in the dormitory. Underneath, each small event shows how prejudice spreads from adults to children, and how quickly words become actions.

Why it matters

Mukasonga writes in short, clear chapters. The calm voice makes the growing danger even more frightening, because the reader sees what the girls cannot yet see.

“Each small event shows how prejudice spreads from adults to children.”

Members of the club agreed that the book should be read in every secondary school: it is short enough to finish in a weekend, and difficult enough to discuss for a month.

Our verdict

Read it slowly, and read it with others. Our next meeting is on Saturday 7 November at the Kigali Public Library.
